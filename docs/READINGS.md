# Two blind readings, in full

The line-up behind "One model, one brief, one idea" in [`EVIDENCE.md`](EVIDENCE.md): three films, read on 13 November 2026 by two
readers from two families of model. Each reader was given the brief in [`EYES.md`](../skills/video-decompiler/EYES.md) word for
word, one added line on the size of the tiles, and one folder of sheets made by `vd pack`: every film at two frames a second,
and every frame of its five busiest seconds. Neither was told what the films were, or that two came from one brief. The
letters were dealt differently for each reader, so each key is given first. Their words are unchanged; only their headings are set two levels lower.

## Reader 1: Claude Opus 5.5

In this reader's line-up, **A** was test 5, **B** was test 3 and **C** was Plus One.

#### 1. Ranking (motion design craft, best first)

**1. A (Larder, ~32 s) · 2. B (Larder, ~31 s) · 3. C (Plus One, ~38.5 s)**

**Why A is above B.** A and B sell the same product with nearly the same script ("What's for dinner…", the list rewriting itself, "Sam's got it."), so they compare directly.
- **Swap scene.** A's swap is one continuous move. The chosen pizza grows out of its thumbnail while the old plate shrinks away left (A move-3 f664–f673). The new title then writes in "Chicken", then "& spinach pizza" (f673–f690). B's swap flickers the hero photo through four dishes in about 1.5 s: burger, a blurred risotto, roast chicken again, then ramen (B story 11.50 → 13.00). It reads as indecision, not a choice.
- **Opening.** A's day pills and plates scroll on a smooth, even pan. Each plate pops in from a small scale and its label fades in a few frames later (A move-1 f256–f305, move-2 f308–f367). B's opening pill roll ends in a pile-up: "Fri / Sat / Wed / Thu / Sun" pills overlap each other at B 3.50s.
- **Crossfades.** B's are muddier. "Roast chicken" shows twice, stacked and ghosted, at B move-2 f488–f490. The phone shrinks into the "15" tile as a semi-transparent double image at B move-4 f1393–f1400.
- **Holds.** B holds the finished grid nearly still from about 23.5 s to 25.6 s (B move-5 f1506–f1537 barely change). A has no comparable dead stretch before its end card.

**Why B is above C.** C is the more ambitious film, and its art-skin carousel is the best idea in the lineup. As motion it is less clean.
- **Fireworks over type.** Bursts run across the "20" and "in." for most of a second (C move-5 f1928–f1945).
- **Crowded phone shot.** Zoe's phone comes in with its label crowded and cut at the top edge. Avatars are stuck behind the phone's top corner, and a blurred "MIRA'S 30TH" is cut off at the right edge (C move-2 f1344–f1355; story 22.50–23.50).
- **Crops after the push-in.** Avatar names ("Mei", "Om") and the top of the card are cut by the frame (C move-4 f1831–f1874).
- **Tiny captions.** The art credits ("Irises · Vincent van Gogh, 1890 · The Met") are too small to read (C 11.50–17.00).
- **Numeral misread.** The serif "1" reads as a capital "I", so the counter says "I … in." (C move-3 f1439–f1478).

B has nothing that cluttered. Its faults are blandness and muddy crossfades, not collisions.

#### 2. What each film sells and what happens

- **A** sells Larder, a weekly meal planner whose shopping list updates itself. A rolling "What's for dinner on MON/TUE/WED…?" turns into a scrolling strip of seven plates. Thursday's spaghetti is swapped for pizza, the shopping list strikes out and adds items, it is sent to Sam's phone and he ticks it off, and the film loops back to the opening question before a logo build.
- **B** sells the same product, Larder, with photo tiles instead of round plates. The day question becomes a two-row grid of dish photos. One day opens, the dish is swapped (roast chicken for miso ramen), the item count runs 14→11→15, the list goes to Sam's phone and gets checked off, and the grid shrinks into the app icon with the line "Seven dinners. One list." and a "Start with Monday" button.
- **C** sells Plus One, a party invitation app. A chaotic group chat ("which saturday", "what time") dissolves. An invite is typed in ("Name it."), given a museum-artwork skin ("Dress it."), and sent to 24 people ("Send it."). A counter of RSVPs climbs from 1 to 20 with fireworks, including a "+1" toggle for a guest's friend Theo, before the logo.

#### 3. Who made each

- **A: freelance motion designer, medium confidence.** The design system is consistent and the choreography is careful throughout. The one thing that keeps it out of "studio" is the unhandled collision at move-4 f745–f757, where the title text runs over the pizza as both shrink. Studio QC catches that.
- **B: freelance motion designer, low-to-medium confidence.** It is competent and clean, but it uses generic moves (fade in, slow push, hold). What pushes me there most is the long static grid hold near the end, plus crossfades that ghost instead of morphing.
- **C: freelance motion designer, low confidence.** The art direction is beyond a hobbyist: per-artwork colour grading and type treatments. What pushes me away from "studio" is the lack of restraint and cleanup: fireworks laid over the headline, avatars hidden behind the phone, credits nobody can read.

#### 4. Strongest moment and weakest stretch

- **A.** Strongest: the swap, 10.6–11.6 s. The plate hands off to the pizza thumbnail and the title retypes word by word. Weakest: 12.3–12.7 s, where the title collides with the pizza. The list's bottom rows also sit half-clipped under the "Send list" button from 12.5 to 18 s.
- **B.** Strongest: the list rewrite, 13.5–16.5 s. Rows highlight in red, the count reads 13, 12, 11, 13, 15, and the cause and effect is clear. Weakest: 23.5–25.7 s, a near-static grid with "Dinner, answered." The swap flicker at 11.5–13.0 s is a close second.
- **C.** Strongest: the art carousel, 11.0–15.5 s. Each card has its own type treatment and the background regrades to match. Weakest: 31.9–33.0 s, where fireworks bury the "20 in." payoff. Also 22.4–23.8 s, the cramped and clipped Zoe's-phone shot.

#### 6. Would any of them embarrass its maker?

None is an outright embarrassment, but two would look worse next to the others.

- **C.** Its fireworks finale (32.0–33.0 s) and the Zoe's-phone shot (22.5–23.5 s) look cluttered and uncleaned next to A's restraint. Viewers would remember the clutter more than the strong carousel.
- **B.** Shown beside A, it reads as the weaker take on the same script. Same product name, same opening question, same "Sam's got it." beat. Its flickering swap and long static grid would be compared beat for beat against A's cleaner versions. That comparison is the embarrassment risk, more than any single frame.
- **A.** It would hold up. Its title-over-pizza collision (12.4–12.6 s) is the one moment a sharp viewer would catch.

## Reader 2: GPT-6.1 Sol

In this reader's line-up, **A** was Plus One, **B** was test 5 and **C** was test 3.

RANKING: A > B > C.

1. RANKING

A above B: A handles more complicated spatial choreography with better continuity. In A/move-1, f758–817 (12.63–13.62s), the invitation carousel changes its foreground card while maintaining depth, overlap and a clear focal plane. The subsequent move from the fireworks artwork into surrounding fireworks gives the decoration a purpose (A/story-02, 16.50–18.00s). B’s plate entrances are controlled, but its transitions are less carefully resolved: the pizza title travels directly across the pizza photograph in B/move-4, f747–756 (12.45–12.60s). A’s main disadvantage is excessive repetition in its second half.

B above C: B gives objects room to move and uses clearer differences in scale. Its selected pizza grows out of the alternatives while the spaghetti and other choices recede (B/move-3, f659–682, 10.98–11.37s). C animates tightly packed cards independently until they collide: the first-row captions and second-row photographs interfere in C/move-1, particularly f389–403 (6.48–6.72s). C also shows two Thursday labels during its card extraction (C/move-2, f477–486, 7.95–8.10s). Those are visible failures of coordination.

I inspected all 25 images. This ranking concerns picture and the supplied frame sequences; it does not establish how the films play with sound.

2. WHAT IS BEING SOLD, AND WHAT HAPPENS

A: Plus One sells attractive digital invitations with guest responses and plus-one handling. A confused chat becomes a named invitation, cycles through visual styles, goes to guests and accumulates RSVPs before resolving into the brand.

B: Larder sells weekly meal planning with a shopping list that updates when dinner changes and can be sent to another person. Seven dinners assemble, spaghetti is replaced by pizza, ingredients change, and Sam receives and uses the list.

C: Larder sells a shared weekly dinner plan connected to an automatically maintained shopping list. A meal-card grid opens into Thursday’s recipe, roast chicken becomes ramen, ingredients are replaced, and Sam receives the list and checks off purchases.

3. WHO I THINK MADE EACH FILM

A: A professional studio. Low-to-moderate confidence; the strongest signal is the coordinated treatment of card depth, artwork, background colour and the fireworks reveal.

B: A freelance motion designer. Moderate confidence; the strongest signal is the economical staging of the recipe swap, which maintains object identity through a substantial layout change.

C: A skilled hobbyist. Low-to-moderate confidence; the strongest signal is the independently animated grid cards colliding with their neighbours, suggesting animation was added without checking the resulting composition closely enough.

These are guesses about the finish, not evidence of authorship.

4. STRONGEST MOMENT AND WEAKEST STRETCH

A:
- Strongest: 15.50–18.00s, A/story-02. The dark invitation takes priority, the surrounding cards withdraw, and fireworks extend the selected artwork into the scene.
- Weakest: 24.00–34.00s, A/story-03. The RSVP count keeps demonstrating something already understood. More badges and fireworks add activity without enough new information.

B:
- Strongest: 13.50–17.50s, B/story-02. Removing the spaghetti ingredients, adding pizza ingredients and increasing the shared chicken quantity make the product benefit concrete.
- Weakest: 22.00–26.00s, B/story-02 and story-03. The film returns to cycling dinners after completing its demonstration. It repeats the opening and introduces mismatched picture-and-caption transition states.

C:
- Strongest: 13.00–16.00s, C/story-02. The chicken ingredients leave and ramen ingredients arrive, visibly connecting the recipe change to the shopping list.
- Weakest: 5.88–8.25s, C/move-1 and move-2. The grid bobs into itself, then Thursday extracts with duplicate labels and briefly doubled text. This is the least finished animation passage in the comparison.

5. PLACES WHERE THE MOTION OR MOVING COMPOSITION LOOKS WRONG

A:
- A/story-01, 6.50s: the rising invitation obscures part of “Name it.” This is an intentional overlap, but the intermediate composition sacrifices the instruction’s readability.
- A/move-3, f1475–1478 (24.58–24.63s), also A/story-03 at 26.00s: travelling guest avatars cross the invitation headline. Their route unnecessarily obstructs the central information.
- A/move-4, approximately f1836–1874 (30.60–31.23s): the close camera framing cuts peripheral avatars and their names at the top and side edges. These are recognisable people and labels, so the cropping feels less controlled than cropping background decoration.
- A/move-5, f1921–1925 (32.02–32.08s): the outgoing “19” and incoming “20” overlap into a doubled numeral. This is an intentional number transition, but its intermediate lettering is muddy.
- A/move-5, f1930–1954 (32.17–32.57s): fireworks spread across the count, guest portraits and parts of the central composition. The celebration competes with the result it should emphasise.
- A/story-03, 24.00–34.00s: repeated counting, badge arrivals and bursts make the motion feel mechanically prolonged. This is a pacing judgment, not a frozen-frame fault.

The tiny rectangles travelling toward guests in A/move-2 read as miniature invitations being sent, not rendering debris.

B:
- B/move-4, f747–756 (12.45–12.60s), especially f749: “Chicken & spinach pizza” crosses the photograph while moving from the right-hand title position to the caption position below. Letterforms disappear against the food. The title and photograph need coordinated paths.
- B/move-4, f757–765 (12.62–12.75s): the shopping panel enters extremely faintly. Its text and controls exist as a washed-out intermediate image before becoming readable. The entrance is continuous, but visually weak.
- B/story-02, 23.00s: a salad photograph appears while the fading caption still says “Chicken & spinach pizza.”
- B/story-03, 25.00s: the chicken-and-beetroot photograph appears while the fading caption still says “Farfalle, olives & rocket,” with “TUE” still visible above. Picture, day and caption are changing on different schedules.

The dishes and day chips passing through the left and right boundaries in B/move-1 and move-2 form a coherent horizontal procession. I would not call those deliberate entrances and exits accidental clipping.

C:
- C/story-01, 3.50s: the gathered weekday pills overlap until several labels are obscured. The gathering is deliberate; the intermediate typography is still a pile-up.
- C/move-1, f385–412 (6.42–6.87s), especially f389–403: independent vertical movement consumes the gutters between rows. The fish caption meets the pizza below, and the beef-taco caption is invaded by the rising paella card. The grid loses both alignment and separation.
- C/move-2, f477–486 (7.95–8.10s): two red “Thu” pills are visible—one remains above the original position while another travels with the extracted card. This reads as a duplicated element.
- C/move-2, f487–491 (8.12–8.18s): the compact and expanded versions of “Roast chicken” overlap during the card transformation. The title briefly looks printed twice.
- C/move-2, approximately f496–503 (8.27–8.38s): the expanding card’s “Swap dish” button is clipped along its lower boundary before the full button settles into view.
- C/move-4, approximately f1394–1407 (23.23–23.45s): the shrinking phone becomes translucent across the returning shopping-summary tile. Two scales of shopping interface overlap, producing a ghosted intermediate composition instead of a clean handoff.

C/story-01 at 11.50–12.50s shows changing hero photographs while the alternatives are open. I cannot tell from those half-second samples whether this is intentional hover previewing or an unwanted image change.

I found no definite accidental one-frame disappearance or large position jump in the fifteen consecutive-frame excerpts. I also found no confirmed completely motionless hold longer than half a second within those excerpts; the near-still closing compositions are deliberate brand holds. The story sheets cannot establish frame continuity between their samples.

I accounted for the reduced tile sizes. I am not treating ordinary interface text as unreadable merely because it is small on these sheets.

6. WOULD ANY EMBARRASS ITS MAKER PUBLICLY SIDE BY SIDE?

C, yes, in a comparison explicitly about motion craft. The grid collisions and duplicated Thursday label make it look insufficiently checked, and B exposes those weaknesses by handling a very similar product demonstration more cleanly.

B has an obvious title-path error worth fixing, but I would not call the whole film embarrassing. A would not embarrass its maker; its main problem is failing to stop the RSVP demonstration soon enough.
